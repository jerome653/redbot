redbot

What this is

in one line

redbot finds Reddit threads where somebody has a WordPress problem, writes a reply, and then tries to prove its own reply wrong. You decide whether any of it goes out.

findrankwritefact-checkyou decideyou send
There is no send button anywhere in here. Sending is a command you type in a terminal, so every word that goes out is one a person chose to put their name on. This screen can only read.

Review — the screen you live on

Their question on the left, in their own words. What redbot made of it on the right.

The verdict comes before the reply on purpose. Reading a well-written reply first makes you judge whether it sounds right instead of whether it is right — that is exactly how a confident, fluent, completely wrong reply nearly got sent.

Do not send means something in the reply was shown to be false. Needs a look means the check could not settle it. Holds up means nothing could be disproved — you still decide.

Argus — the part that tries to prove the reply wrong

Argus is the fact-checker. Everything before it is trying to write something good; Argus is the only step trying to show that what was written is false. It is where Do not send, Needs a look and Holds up come from.

Why it exists. One reply got all the way to the end of the queue: well written, specific, careful, and it said something about databases that is simply not true. Every other check passed it, because every other check can only measure how the writing reads. Nothing was measuring whether it was right. Argus is the answer to that one reply.

What it actually does. It never judges the reply as a piece of writing. It pulls the reply apart into individual statements — one fact each, no "and" — and then puts every statement through the same questions:

Has this already been solved? If the person who asked has said they fixed it, the check stops there. No amount of quality makes a reply to a finished conversation worth sending.

Where did this come from? Every statement has to say what backs it: documentation, the actual code, something observed happening, or common practice. A statement that cannot say where it came from does not go out.

Can it be disproved? Each statement is attacked on purpose — the question asked is "show me this is wrong", never "is this okay". A statement nobody tried to disprove is one that marked its own homework.

Does the wording match the evidence? A guess written in the voice of a fact fails here, even when the guess turns out to be right. This deliberately disagrees with the writing check, which rewards sounding sure.

Does anything rest on a broken piece? If one statement falls, everything built on top of it falls with it. There is no keeping the good half of a broken argument.

Do we have written material on this? Where we keep our own reference material on a subject — anything about our own product — a statement has to line up with it. A statement about our own product that no card covers is one we made up. This finds cards on the same subject; it cannot tell a card that agrees from one that disagrees, so it hands them to you to read rather than deciding.

The model writes; the rules decide. A model is used to break the reply into statements and to attack them, because that needs language. Every yes-or-no after that is plain code following fixed rules — so the thing being checked never gets a vote on whether it passed.

What it cannot do. It cannot tell you a reply is true. Everything it checks is a shape it can see in the words: where a statement claims to come from, how sure it sounds, whether an attack found something. A statement can be well-sourced, carefully worded, unrefuted — and wrong. The last check is you.

Threads — how one gets chosen

You collect them by hand: read a subreddit, or run a search. Up to 15 a time. Then each one has to survive six filters:

1. Is it a question? Anything tagged Guide, PSA, Showcase, Promo, Megathread and the like is dropped on the title alone. So is anyone shopping for a product.

2. Is it still live? Over 72 hours old and it is dropped — measured from right now, not from when it was collected.

3. What has already been said? Every claim already in the thread gets listed, and the holes get named: a wrong claim nobody corrected, a part nobody answered, a question nobody thought to ask, an answer that stops short, a diagnosis nobody can confirm.

4. Can we actually fill the hole? Checked against the thread's own words. Generic hosting and database terms only count when WordPress is genuinely in the picture.

5. Score it. A wrong claim standing uncorrected is worth most, then a missing diagnostic question, then something never answered. What is already well covered is penalised, so a thread with twelve good answers does not win.

6. Is it worth a stranger appearing? Below 40 it is skipped. 40 is exactly one unanswered part that we can genuinely answer, and nothing else.

Out of 58 threads collected, 24 got this far, 17 were worth answering. Skip is meant to be the common answer — anything that finds an opportunity in every thread has not found one.

Accounts — who would be posting

Only accounts that have actually been measured appear here. A browser sign-in sitting on this machine is not proof of an account, so the two numbers are shown separately rather than smoothed into one.

Karma under 10 is the state Reddit's filters are built to catch. Comments can be removed or held back quietly — still visible to you while signed in, invisible to everybody else.

Growing — why nothing is happening yet

Both accounts are new. Getting them to where a reply survives is roughly six weeks of comments written by a person, two to four a day, no links and no product names.

redbot cannot speed this up. Karma comes from other people finding comments useful. It can find threads, write, keep the pace slow and record what gets taken down — that is all.

Results — empty on purpose

Karma on a comment, replies to it, whether a moderator removed it: all of it only exists after something is sent. Nothing has been sent, so there is nothing here.

That is an empty screen, not a screen full of zeroes. The difference matters — a zero would mean measured and none, and nothing here has been measured.
